System Guide & Troubleshooting

How FindFlower handles scanning, server states, local data, and routing.

Nearly everything that goes wrong here is one of four things. The model was asleep. The browser you are in is not the browser your scans are in. A link points at a page that no longer exists. Or there is no connection at all. This page is those four written out, with the numbers the code actually uses rather than round ones.

It is a troubleshooting page and not an explanation of the model. How it works covers that, and Privacy covers what becomes of a photo after you send it. If the site behaves differently from what is written below, this page is the thing that is out of date, and it is worth telling me.

The status dot

There is a small dot above the upload frame on the scan page with a word or two beside it. It answers one question, asked once when the page loads: did the machine that runs the model reply to a health check. The page gives that question 8 seconds and then stops waiting. Four answers are possible.

Checking scanner…
Where the page starts, with the check still out. You will rarely catch it for more than a fraction of a second.
Ready to identify
The reply came back inside 1.5 seconds, so the model is already loaded. A photo sent now comes back in a second or two.
Waking up the model…
The reply took longer than 1.5 seconds, or it came back as a server error. The host puts the model to sleep when nobody has used it for a while, and whoever arrives next pays for waking it up. Send the photo anyway. It goes through. It is just slow.
Scanner offline
Nothing came back at all. Usually that is the connection at your end. If everything else on the internet loads, the service is restarting, and a few minutes plus a reload is the whole fix. Scans you already have sit in this browser and read normally while you wait.

Why a scan sometimes takes a minute

A model that is awake answers in about a second. One that has been asleep has to load its weights before it can look at anything, and that is the wait people notice and reasonably assume is a bug. So the page keeps changing what it says on a fixed schedule instead of spinning in silence.

Straight away
The photo has gone and the page says it is analysing.
At 6 seconds
Longer than a warm request takes. The wording changes to warn you a first try can be slow.
At 20 seconds
This is a cold start. The page says so rather than pretending everything is normal.
At 45 seconds
Slower than a cold start usually runs. Still worth waiting out.
At 95 seconds
The request is cancelled and you get an error.

Try once more before deciding it is broken. The attempt that failed normally leaves the model awake behind it, so the second one is quick.

Your photo does not go straight to the host. It passes through a small proxy on the way, so a request can be stuck in the middle while both ends are perfectly healthy. The API reference has the endpoint and the response shape if you would rather skip the page and call it yourself.

What an account does

An account here does less than an account usually does, and the gap between what people expect and what it actually is causes more confusion than anything else on the site. So, plainly.

Scanning does not need one
The scanner works signed out, and a scan made as a guest is saved exactly the way a signed-in one is. Nothing is held back for members.
What it actually changes
It keeps records apart. Scans made while you are signed in are filed under your account, so two people sharing a laptop do not end up sharing a history. It also gets you a handle to post with in the community. Auth0 runs the sign-in itself, so FindFlower never sees your password.
It does not carry your history to another device
This is the one to remember. Scans, thumbnails, streak and badges are written to this browser and nowhere else. Sign in on your phone and you get the same account with an empty history, because there is no copy on a server to pull down. The sign-in page says as much before you commit to it.
Your data comes out as a file
The dashboard writes the whole lot to a JSON file in your downloads. Keep it, or read it yourself. There is no import to match it, so it will not refill another browser for you.

Where each thing is kept

Data lives in two places. Almost every message about a record that has vanished turns out to be a question of which of the two it was in.

Scans, thumbnails
A browser database named findflower, on this machine. Photos are shrunk to 320 pixels on the long edge before being stored, so a long history stays small. Streaks and species counts are added up from those rows each time a page needs them. Delete a scan and the numbers move immediately, because nothing was being kept on the side.
Unsent feedback
Local storage in this browser, until you decide to send it. Nothing goes anywhere on its own.
Community post text
The FindFlower server. The one thing on this list that leaves your machine on purpose. It has to, or nobody else could read what you wrote, and for the same reason it is the one thing that does show up on your other devices.
Photos on a post
Not uploaded. A post carries its text; the identification behind it and its thumbnail stay local.

Clear your site data, or open FindFlower in another browser, another profile, or a private window, and the local half of that list is empty. Posts you have already published come back as normal. They were never sitting here to begin with.

A post that will not send is nearly always one of two things: you are signed out, or you have not claimed a handle yet. The community server sleeps when it is idle as well, so the feed can sit empty for a moment on the first load after a quiet spell. The community page goes into its own storage in more detail.

Links, 404s and page changes

FindFlower is a folder of static files. /pricing is served out of pricing.html, and both spellings of the address land on the same page. Where there is no file behind a path you get the not-found page, which has a search on it that works. A typo, or a link from an older version of the site, accounts for almost all of those.

Not every dead end is a 404, and the difference tells you where to look.

A species page that shows almost nothing
Species profiles are filled in from Wikipedia and Wikidata as you open them rather than being written into the page in advance. When a name cannot be matched there, the profile loads with whatever it did find and offers a Wikipedia search instead. Thin, but not broken.
An article link that says there is no entry
Written entries are looked up by an id in the address. An id that no longer exists gets you that message on the article page instead of a 404, with a link back to everything that has been published.
A scan that has gone from your history
Check you are in the browser, and the profile, that made it. Scans are never fetched from anywhere. A missing one means you are looking at a different local database than the one you are thinking of.

Moving between pages does not usually reload the browser. The click is caught in the page, the next document is fetched, and its main content is swapped in with the title and the address brought along to match. If any part of that fails the site stops being clever and does an ordinary full load of the same address, so the worst case is a slower page change rather than a blank screen. Two pages always load in full. The scanner, because it is holding a live camera stream, and sign-in, because it has to catch a redirect back from Auth0. Neither survives being swapped in halfway.

Working without a connection

You cannot use FindFlower offline. Nothing is cached for it.

Opening or reloading any page needs the network, and a scan needs it twice over, because the model runs on a server rather than in your browser. Better to read that here than to work it out halfway up a hillside with no signal.

Nothing you have saved is lost in the meantime. It is sitting in the browser database and reads back as usual the moment a page can load again.
