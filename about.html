About FindFlower

FindFlower is a hobby project. It is not a company, and it is not trying to become one.

It is built and maintained by gsor56, a student, alone, in evenings and weekends. Nobody is paid for it. There is no team, no funding, no roadmap meeting, and no plan to sell anything. When something on this site breaks, one person fixes it, which is also why it sometimes takes a while.

It started with a plant nobody nearby could name. The obvious answer was to photograph it and ask an app, and that turned out to mean an account, a subscription prompt, three permissions dialogs and a feed. That is the thing this is a reaction to more than any technical ambition.

What it is not

This needs saying plainly, because a website with a favicon and a beta badge tends to be read as a business.

FindFlower is not a registered company, a startup, a partnership, or any kind of legal entity.

It is a personal, non-commercial project by a private individual. There is no incorporation anywhere, no employees, no shareholders, and no commercial relationship with iNaturalist, Trefle, Hugging Face, or any other service it happens to use. Names of those services appear here as description, not as endorsement or partnership.

Nothing here is professional advice. Identifications are a model’s best guess and can be confidently wrong. Do not eat, brew, apply, or medicate with a plant on the strength of a scan. The Terms of Service say the same thing in more careful language, and mean it.

Pricing exists on this site because the API has running costs and a paid tier is the honest way to cover them if it ever gets used enough to have any. It is not a business model.

What it is for

An app you can point at a flower on a walk, that answers, and then leaves you alone. Three commitments hold that together, and each one is a real constraint on what gets built rather than a slogan.

No ads, no trackers, no analytics

No analytics at all, not even the “privacy-respecting” kind. There is no tag manager, no pixel, no third-party script counting your visit. Which means nobody knows how many people use this, and that is a cost worth paying.

Your scans stay on your device

Saved identifications, photographs, streaks and settings live in your own browser’s storage. There is no copy on a server, which also means there is no sync: clear your browser data and the history is gone, and it will not be waiting on another phone.

One thing is sent, and only for a moment

Identification runs on a server, so the photograph you scan is uploaded to be classified. It is not stored, not logged with your identity, and not attached to anything afterwards. Why it works that way, and what the alternative would have cost, is written out in Research.

How it is built

Plainly, and on purpose. The site is HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript with no framework and no build step, so what is in the repository is what the browser runs. A student project that needs a toolchain reinstalled to fix a typo two years from now is a project that quietly dies.

The application code is closed. That is not a philosophical position, it is a solo maintainer declining to also run a public codebase. The data is a different matter: the curation method, the thresholds and the class taxonomy are all written down, and the dataset is meant to be usable by other people.

Reporting a problem

A wrong identification is genuinely useful to hear about, more useful than a compliment, since it points at a class that needs better images. So is a broken page, a confusing button, or a species you expected the model to know.

One person reads everything that comes in, so replies are not instant. They do happen.
