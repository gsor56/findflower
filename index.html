How it works

Three steps, about ten seconds

  1. 01

    Take or upload a photo

    Point the camera at a bloom, pick a photo already on your phone, or paste a link to one. One clear shot of the flower is enough, and close beats wide.

  2. 02

    Get a name and a score

    The species comes back with a confidence figure beside it and the next closest matches underneath. A low figure means it is unsure, which is worth seeing before you believe it.

  3. 03

    Read the record

    Family, growth habit, how much light and water it wants, a reference photo and a link to the full article. Anything a source has no data for is left out rather than guessed.

Why this one

It only does flowers

No leaf keys, no bird calls, no general plant scanner with flowers bolted on the side. Flowering species photographed outdoors is the one thing it tries to be good at.

Free while it is in beta

No trial to start, no card to enter, no upgrade prompt halfway through a scan. What might eventually cost money is already written down.

No account to start

Open the scanner and use it. Putting it on your home screen is a choice rather than a step. Scans you keep are stored in this browser rather than on a server, which also means they will not be waiting for you on another phone. Where each thing is kept.

It admits when it is unsure

Every answer carries a confidence figure and its runners-up, because a wrong name given confidently is worse than an uncertain one. Model versions are listed as they ship.

About FindFlower

FindFlower is an independent project rather than a company, and it is not trying to become one. It is free to use, and the parts that are still missing are described rather than hidden.

There are no ads, no trackers and no analytics of any kind, which has a real cost: nobody here knows how many people use this, and that is the trade being made.

Common questions

How do I find out what a flower is?

Take a photo of it, or pick one you already have, and drop it into the scanner. Back comes the species name, a figure for how sure the model is, and the botanical record that name belongs to. One flower filling most of the frame works best, because outdoor photographs like that are what the model learned from.

Is this a web app, or do I have to install something?

It is a web page. It opens in the browser you already have, on a phone or a laptop, and there is nothing in any app store. The menu carries an Install app row when your browser offers one, and after that it opens in its own window with no address bar. On an iPhone the same thing is Add to Home Screen in the Safari share sheet.

Does it work without a signal?

Half of it does, and it is worth knowing which half. Opening or reloading a page needs a connection, and with the signal gone you land on our own no connection notice rather than a browser error. The smaller model that runs on your own device is a scanner preference on the dashboard, and setting it does need a sign-in, but once it is on the identifying happens inside the tab and your photo never leaves the phone. The written record that follows a result is fetched, so that part waits until you are back in range.

Which flowers does it know?

116 species on the model that runs by default, and 107 on the smaller one that runs on your device. It is a fixed list of flowering plants rather than everything green, so a leaf, a tree or a vegetable will not get a useful answer out of it. The whole list is in the directory, alongside thousands more species you can read about without scanning anything.
