How it works

How identification works

  1. 01

    Capture or upload

    Point your camera at a plant, or drop in a photo you already have. One clear frame of a bloom or a leaf is enough. No install and no account needed.

  2. 02

    The model reads it

    The photo is sent to a Vision Transformer running under ONNX Runtime, which returns a species and a confidence score. The image is used for that prediction and is not stored afterwards. The privacy page sets out what happens to it.

  3. 03

    Read the record

    Wikidata supplies the binomial and family, Trefle the growth habit and light needs, Wikipedia the reference photo and article. Those four requests assemble into the record shown here.

About FindFlower

FindFlower names the plant in a photo. Submit one image and it returns a species, a confidence score, and the botanical record behind them.

The confidence score and the next-best matches are shown next to the name so you can judge how reliable the identification is.

Records are assembled from Wikidata, Trefle and Wikipedia when you request them. Fields a source has no data for are left out rather than filled with a guess.

Architecture

What runs it

Classifier

A Vision Transformer exported to ONNX and served through ONNX Runtime, covering 116 botanical classes. Each prediction returns a confidence score and its closest alternatives.

Species data

Wikidata for taxonomy, Trefle for growing conditions, Wikipedia for the reference photo and article. Each source is queried when you open a record, so nothing is served from a stale copy.

Accounts

Not required. Open the scanner and identify. Scan history is kept in your own browser and you can clear it at any time.
