How it works

How FindFlower works

Three steps, no account, and a confidence score on every prediction.

Identify a flower

Three steps,
start to finish

Step 01

Scan or upload

Use the camera, drop in a photo you already have, or paste an image link. One clear shot of a bloom or a leaf is enough, and no account is involved at any point.

Step 02

The model ranks what it sees

The photo goes to the inference endpoint and comes back with the most likely species, its closest runners-up, and a confidence score. A low score means it is unsure, and the runners-up are shown for exactly that reason.

Step 03

Wikipedia fills in the rest

Each match pulls a short summary and a reference image from Wikipedia, with a link to the full article.

What runs the
identification

A fine-tuned Vision Transformer

Trained on a curated flower set and served as ONNX. It returns ranked candidates with a confidence score for each.

116 botanical classes

Anything outside those classes comes back as the nearest match it knows, which is why the runners-up are always shown.

A hosted endpoint, not your device

The weights stay on the server. The photo is uploaded, analysed, and dropped.

Corrections

What happens when you say it got it wrong

Every result can be marked right or wrong. That verdict is written to this browser and nowhere else. Nothing is uploaded, and nobody reads it.

The pipeline that would train on those corrections does not exist yet. When it does, it will be described here and on the releases page before a single record is collected. Until then the log belongs to you. Your dashboard counts it, exports it, and erases it on request.

New model versions in the meantime come from the training set, not from your feedback. Each one is written down.

Your photo

Where the image goes

The photo is sent to the inference endpoint, used to produce the prediction, and not kept afterwards. Only the species name and the confidence score come back. Nothing else about the request is stored.

Scans you save stay in this browser, and the privacy page spells out each of those paths in full.
